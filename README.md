# A collection of written coursework from UC Berkeley CS 189/289A. Spring 2026

This collection shows 3 levels of understanding in machine learning I learned in CS 189 at UCBerkeley.

## Mathematical Derivation
    deriving objectives, gradients, Hessians, convergence bounds, and
optimization algorithms from first principles.

Examples include:

- Matrix calculus and vectorized derivatives
- Gradient and Hessian derivations
- Newton's method for logistic regression
- Taylor expansions and smoothness analysis
- Convex optimization and Polyak–Łojasiewicz (PL) conditions
- Convergence-rate analysis
- Computational complexity of optimization algorithms
- Probability, entropy, KL divergence, and mutual information

## Algorithmic Understanding
    connecting mathematical structure to algorithms such as Newton's method,
coordinate descent, backpropagation, implicit differentiation, contrastive
learning, and diffusion models.

With topics including:

- Gradient Descent and Coordinate Descent
- Newton's Method
- Backpropagation
- Reverse-mode automatic differentiation
- Vector-Jacobian Products (VJPs)
- Implicit Differentiation
- Contrastive Learning
- Diffusion Models
- Flow Matching


## Paper-Level Understanding
    studying influential architectures such as ResNet and Transformers by
examining the problems they were designed to solve, their mathematical
mechanisms, and their computational trade-offs.

For Transformers, I analyze:

- Self-attention
- Query / Key / Value representations
- Scaled dot-product attention
- Multi-head attention
- Causal masking
- Positional encoding
- Encoder-decoder attention
- Parallelization and long-range dependencies
